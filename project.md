# Project Logistics
{: .no_toc }

The semester-long project is the largest component of the grade (50%). It is your
chance to go deep on one corner of the course material.

## Scope

Anything that builds upon or applies optimal transport or flow matching: extending a
method to a new problem, or applying an existing method to new data. The method
can come from the lectures, from a paper on the reading list, or from somewhere else
entirely, and the application can be to any domain you care about. What matters is not
the topic but that you have a real question and go after it properly.

## What your project has to do

1. **Ask an interesting question.** State it plainly and early.
2. **Position it.** How novel is this question? It does not have to be novel at all,
   but you do have to know, and you have to map your question to the closest existing
   literature.
3. **Describe how you answer it**, either with a tool you propose, or by applying
   something that already exists.
4. **Evaluate how well it works, with actual numbers**: use existing benchmarks, or
   propose your own.
5. **Compare against baselines.** You cannot tell how well something you built works
   without quantitatively comparing it to something sensible: a simple method, an
   ablation, or a competing method.
6. **State the limitations** of what you did, and what you would do next.

Negative results are fine. Sometimes a project does not work out. Say so clearly,
show the numbers, and explain what you learned.

## Deliverables

**A poster**, presented in class on Mon Dec 7, one per team. The session runs as a
poster session: your team stands by its poster and talks through the work with whoever
comes by. There is no written report.

Upload the poster as a PDF on [Brightspace](https://brightspace.nyu.edu) by
**Mon Dec 7**, and put a one-line contribution statement on the poster saying who did
what.
